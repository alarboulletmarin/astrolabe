# Navigation

## The rail

The rail on the left opens each page, top to bottom: **Today**, **Journal**, **Calendar**, **Statistics**, **Form**, **Records**, **Heatmap**, **Loops**. At the bottom, the round button shows the [synchronisation](sync.md) (a click starts one, or opens the settings), then **Settings**. Pointing at an icon shows its name.

An [activity](activity.md) has no icon of its own: the **Journal** one stays lit.

## Back and forward

The two arrows at the top of the rail (**Back**, **Forward**) go back and forward through the pages shown, as in a browser, and a page comes back at the scroll you left it at.

- `Alt + ←` and `Alt + →` do the same, as do the mouse's back and forward buttons. On macOS: `Cmd + [` and `Cmd + ]`.
- Going to a new page forgets what lay ahead. A click on the page already shown moves nothing.
- Moving from one activity to another replaces the entry instead of adding one.
- Up to 100 pages are kept, in memory only: nothing survives a restart.

## The keyboard

Every shortcut is in [Shortcuts](shortcuts.md). The main ones: `↑` `↓` and `Enter` in the journal, `←` `→` and `Esc` in an activity, `←` `→` and `T` in the calendar, `Ctrl + L` (`Cmd + L` on macOS) for [Loops](loops.md).

## The window

Astrolabe draws its own frame under Linux. Drag the strip at the top, or hold `Alt` and drag from anywhere, to move it; double-click the strip to maximise or restore it; right-click it for the window menu.
